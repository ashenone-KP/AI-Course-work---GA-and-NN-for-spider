DESIGN OF THE SPIDER GAIT GA (version 2)
=======================================

1. What changed from version 1, and why
---------------------------------------
Version 1 (archive/spider_cw_v1) scored every gait on a fast, no-physics model of the lecturer's
MATLAB spider: a foot was "on the ground" if it was the lowest, the body was carried by feet
sweeping backwards, and stability was the body centre inside the support polygon. After a lot of
tuning it evolved gaits that walked well in that model. Played on the Webots spider, they did not:
    * The two bodies differ: Webots legs are 0.5/0.6/0.7 m (MATLAB 1.2/0.7/1.0), point straight
      out sideways from a 1.3 x 0.4 m box, the hip axes are tilted 45 deg and the joint limits
      differ. The same angles put the feet elsewhere, and 10-44% of joint targets were clamped.
    * Re-evolving against a model of the Webots geometry still failed: with no mass, friction
      or contact, the GA found steps that barely lifted the feet (they scraped in Webots).
Lesson: a fitness function that does not measure the real thing gets exploited. Version 2
therefore walks every gait in Webots physics, with self-collision on, and scores what happened.
Even then, each loophole the physics allowed was found (sections 3 and 6).

2. Encoding (part1_ga/gait.py)
------------------------------
Default: SHARED MOTION, 11 genes. Every leg makes the same step; only its hip direction differs.
    s[0:4]  hip pose of L1..L4 (the fan-out: front legs forward, hind legs back)
    s[4:6]  thigh and knee pose, shared      s[6:9]  hip, thigh, knee swing, shared
    s[9:11] thigh and knee phase, shared
expand_shared() turns it into the per-leg form below (26 genes); decode() accepts either.
Why: with a swing per leg, the GA always let some legs idle while others worked. In the 4-cycle
measurements, legs0-2 each had one pair on the floor only 6-24% of the time, while every leg
of shared0-2 was down 51-82%. Shared motion was also better on 2 of 3 seeds (best 2.48 m vs
1.66 m over 4 cycles) and slid less per metre forward (section 6). The idea came from watching
the per-leg gait in Webots: "all the legs are the same, so match their movements".
(Version 1, no-physics model: sharing the swing made every seed stand still. In physics, with
the per-leg stepping terms, it does not.)

PUSH-PULL (encoding=pushpi, 12 genes: shared motion + the hind pairs' knee phase offset, fixed at pi).
Also the user's idea: the front legs should pull and the hind legs push. With shared motion every
knee does the same thing at the same point of its step, but the front legs point forward and the
hind legs backward, so drawing a foot in while it is down pulls the body forward with a front leg
and backward with a hind leg: the two ends work against each other. Offsetting the hind pairs'
(L3, L4, R4, R3) knee timing by pi makes them extend while the front knees draw in.
    * As a free gene (encoding=pushpull, 6 runs) the GA did not find pi: 4 of 6 ended near 0
      (= plain shared motion), best 1.86 m over 4 cycles. A free circular gene in a small
      population mostly drifts back to the shared solution it starts closest to.
    * Fixed at pi (encoding=pushpi) it gave the best near-level walk: pi0 2.05 m at ~3 deg of
      tilt, against 1.34 m for the best shared gait under the same tilt rule (section 6).
    The thigh timing (the foot lift) stays shared, so all legs still lift in the same rhythm.

STEER (encoding=steer, 13 genes: push-pull with pi forced + one steer gene in [-0.3, 0.3]).
Left hips swing (1 + steer) x the hip amplitude, right hips (1 - steer), so one side takes longer
strides; positive steer turns right. Mirroring makes every other encoding left/right symmetric,
so it has no way to correct a curve that the physics adds; this gene is that way (section 7).
With steer 0 it decodes to exactly the push-pull gait (gait.py self-check).

Per-leg form, 26 real genes (encoding=legs) -> 300 frames x 24 motor angles (legs L1..L4,
R4..R1; hip, thigh, knee).
    angle_j(t) = clip(offset_j + amp_j * sin(2 pi t / 300 + leg_phase + joint_phase_j), limits)
    genes 0-11   base pose of the left legs; right legs mirror them (R = -L for all 3 joints)
    genes 12-23  swing amplitudes, mirrored the same way
    genes 24-25  thigh and knee phase relative to the leg's own hip
    leg_phase = LR * is_right + FB * position (position L1..L4 = 0..3, R4..R1 = 3..0)
    LR = FB = pi fixed: the alternating tetrapod of real spiders, {L1,L3,R2,R4} then {L2,L4,R1,R3}
Why:
    * One sine per joint: smooth and periodic, and a search space small enough to converge.
    * Coordinated timing (version 1, MATLAB model): 72 genes with 24 free phases -> every seed
      stood still; 2000 random phase sets with a working stride, none beat standing. Encoding
      the timing as a wave made every chromosome coordinated.
    * Mirror symmetry (version 1): 0/5 seeds walked without it, 5/8 with it.
    * LR fixed (version 1): with LR as a gene the GA got stuck on left/right pairs moving
      together ("rowing").
    * FB fixed (version 2): with FB as a gene the first physics run chose a quarter-cycle wave
      (fb = 0.52 pi) that looked out of sync, with the front legs pulling and the 3rd pair dragged.
Bounds (left legs; the right side is mirrored):
    * hip pose +-1.2 rad, hip swing <= 0.6. The hips are only 0.325 m apart and the legs point
      straight out, so in the tetrapod neighbouring legs swing towards each other. Measured
      (body.py): collision-free hip swing is 0.15 rad with the legs straight out, 0.35 rad when
      fanned to +-0.6, 0.55 rad at +-1.2. With a +-0.6 limit the GA kept hip swing near 0 and
      stepped in place (0.15-0.37 m in 4 s); with +-1.2 the best run walks 1.87 m in 4 s.
    * thigh pose in [-1.5, 0] (legs below the body); knee pose in [-0.5, 1] so the mirrored
      right knee stays inside the motor range [-2, 1]. Phases wrap.

3. Fitness (controllers/ga_trainer, fitness())
----------------------------------------------
Each gait: restore the saved standing start state, 1.5 s to move into the gait's first pose,
then 2 cycles of 2 s. Every physics step the supervisor records the body position and orientation
and the 24 joint sensors; feet are placed with body.forward_kinematics of the MEASURED angles
(exact for rigid links, and it sees legs blocked by self-collision).
    score = forward - 0.5 |drift| - turnw |turn|          (turnw 0.5; 5 for the straight family, section 7)
            - 5 x (fraction of the walk with the body centre below 0.25 m) - 2 x (dip below it)
            - 5 if it tipped past 60 degrees (evaluation stops)
            - 3 x (fraction of frames whose commanded pose makes legs touch)
            + 0.5 x mean foot lift (capped at 10 cm) of the legs that step
            - 0.4 per leg that does not step (touch the floor AND lift >= 5 cm)
            - 0.3 per metre the feet slide while on the floor
            - 3 x mean body tilt beyond 4 deg (rad, nose up or down); trainer option pitch=0 removes it
Each term answers a failure seen in Webots:
    * forward is the goal; drift and turn keep it straight - over the 2 scored cycles only,
      which was not enough (section 7).
    * Body height: with only a 2x dip penalty the first runs slid forwards on their bellies
      (lowest body height 0.14 m; the box is 0.3 tall). With the time-below penalty all runs
      keep the body >= 0.25 m.
    * Per-leg terms: the next run walked 0.85 m per cycle but only the front pair pulled; the
      3rd pair never lifted (1-2 cm) and was dragged 3.5-4 m over 4 cycles, and the feet slid
      17 m for 3.2 m forward. Now every leg must step, lifting is rewarded up to 10 cm, and
      sliding costs 0.3 per metre. That weight started at 0.1, where run1 still slid 11 m for
      3.8 m forward; at 0.3 the shared-motion gaits slide about 1.9 m per metre forward instead
      of 3. It is not higher because sine joint motion always skates somewhat, and a large
      weight would make standing still the best option.
    * Body tilt: shared1 walked nose-down 12 deg the whole time, which looked wrong in Webots
      ("leans forward a tad too much"). Penalising all tilt levelled the walk to ~1 deg but
      halved the speed (level0-2, best 1.24 m vs 2.48 m): the lean was helping it push. With
      4 deg free (tilt0-2) the best shared gait reached 1.34 m; push-pull then recovered 2.05 m.
* Self-collision: Webots enforces it physically (selfCollision TRUE). In addition,
  part1_ga/body.py checks every commanded pose: leg segments as capsules of the proto's
  thickness against the next two legs and the body box. Gaits colliding in > 20% of frames are
  not simulated (saves time), the rest pay 3 x the colliding fraction.
* Physics is deterministic (the self-check walks one gait 3 times: identical to 1e-6, and the
  live run reproduced the headless run exactly), so scores are cached per chromosome.

4. GA (part1_ga/ga.py) and operator experiments in physics
----------------------------------------------------------
Operators: tournament selection (k=3), blend crossover (BLX-0.5, circular for phases), Gaussian
mutation (per-gene rate 0.05; sigma adaptive: decays with generations and halves when the best
stalls), elitism 2, population 40. All are trainer options (selection=, crossover=, adaptive=,
mrate=, pop=), and part1_ga/ga.py's self-check validates them on test functions first.

They were compared in physics (part1_ga/operator_experiments.py; outputs/operators/): each variant
changes one setting of the reference setup (push-pull encoding, full fitness incl. the tilt rule,
40 x 60 generations), 3 seeds each, every gait walked in Webots with self-collision.
    final best fitness (mean +- std over 3 seeds) | forward distance of the best gait (2-cycle test)
    selection   tournament           +0.35 +- 0.30   0.76 m
                roulette             +0.28 +- 0.08   0.45 m
    crossover   blend                +0.35 +- 0.30   0.76 m
                uniform              +0.35 +- 0.25   0.35 m
                two-point            -0.09 +- 0.57   0.38 m
    mutation    adaptive, rate 0.05  +0.35 +- 0.30   0.76 m
                fixed sigma          +0.48 +- 0.15   0.66 m
                rate 0.01            -0.25 +- 0.28   0.37 m
                rate 0.2             +0.51 +- 0.09   0.96 m
    population  20                   +0.14 +- 0.51   0.84 m
                40                   +0.35 +- 0.30   0.76 m
                80                   +0.68 +- 0.43   0.89 m
What it shows (3 seeds, so differences within one std are not conclusive):
* Selection: tournament reaches the best gaits (0.76 m vs 0.45 m); roulette is steadier but its
  pressure is weak late on, when scores are close and some are negative (they are shifted
  before the roulette wheel), so it stops improving. Tournament only compares ranks, so it keeps
  constant pressure whatever the scale - the reason it is the default.
* Crossover: two-point is clearly worst (lowest mean, largest spread). It cuts the chromosome into
  blocks, but the hip fan, shared posture, swing and phase genes only work together. Blend and
  uniform tie on fitness; blend's best gait walks twice as far (0.76 vs 0.35 m). Blend samples
  between and around two good parents, so children stay coherent: kept as the default. The same
  ranking (blend first, two-point last) was found in version 1 on the no-physics model.
* Mutation rate is the biggest effect: 0.01 barely explores and stalls (all seeds below 0);
  0.2 is best and the most consistent. With only 12 genes, rate 0.05 mutates 0.6 genes per child
  on average, so most children are pure crossover; 0.2 mutates ~2.4. Version 1 found 0.05 best,
  but that encoding had 28 genes (1.4 per child): the right per-gene rate scales with 1/genes.
  A fixed sigma also beat the adaptive one in these short runs: the adaptive sigma decays
  linearly over the run, and in 60 generations it shrinks before the search has explored.
* Population: 80 is best, 20 worst and least reliable (one seed ended negative); 80 costs twice
  the simulation time of 40.
Conclusion: for this 12-gene encoding a higher mutation rate (~0.2) and, if time allows, a larger
population would be better defaults than the version-1 values. The reference gaits (pi0 etc.)
were evolved with the old defaults over 100 generations; the operators above were not re-tuned
for them.

5. Visualisation
----------------
* Live: the trainer labels the Webots view with the generation, current evaluation and best
  distance, and replays the best gait of every generation in real time.
* measure=<run> (trainer option): walks a saved gait and prints, per leg, the real foot lift,
  time on the floor, slide distance and whether it steps; and the path: distance walked, sideways
  offset, heading change, curvature (deg per metre), turning radius and how far it bows from a
  straight line. The path is saved as outputs/<run>/track.csv; outputs/gait_paths.png plots the
  20-cycle paths of the 13 main gaits on one figure.
* replay=<run> (trainer option): walks the best gait of every generation where the best score
  improved, in real time, captioned with generation, score, distance, tilt and legs stepping,
  and records it as outputs/<run>/learning.mp4 (pi0: 30 improvements, ~3 min; the video is not
  in the repository, it is 208 MB).
* worlds/all_gaits.wbt: the 13 main gaits (GA references, other configurations, the best operator
  run, two network gaits) walking side by side in 15 m lanes, with heading feedback.
* part1_ga/plots.py on a saved run: best/mean/worst fitness, each measured component of the
  best, mutation sigma, the best pose at 4 points in the run, joint angles per leg, commanded
  foot heights with a footfall diagram, and a GIF.

7. Walking straight
-------------------
Found by watching all gaits side by side (worlds/all_gaits.wbt): some walked into the next lane.
Measured over 20 cycles (40 s; measure= with cycles=20), most GA gaits curve:
    gait            heading change   turning radius   sideways at the end
    pifree0          -0.0 deg         ~50 km           -0.22 m
    shared1          +1.6             464 m            +0.41
    pi0              -3.7             134 m            -0.46
    ref10_2         +28.6             21 m             +2.60
    pop80_s2        -63.7             20 m            -11.70   (outputs/operators, best operator run)
    run1             +0.5             2 km             +1.14   (crabs sideways without turning)
Why the GA did not stop it: each gait is scored over 2 cycles, where ref10_2 turned about 1 deg
and pop80_s2 under 2 deg; the drift and turn terms (0.5 per m and per rad) cost almost nothing.
The curve only shows over longer walks. The speed family refined from ref10_2 (speed015-090, the
network's first training data, nn_design.txt section 2) inherited it: all 9 curved left,
heading +1.6 to +33 deg over 20 cycles, radius 17-180 m, and so did the network trained on it.

Fix, in three steps:
1. Steer gene (section 2). Without it the encoding has no way to steer.
2. Re-evolve each speed gait with it (encoding=steer, start=speed0xx, target = its measured
   4-cycle distance, cycles=4, turnw=5, 40 generations; outputs/straight0xx). Every result turned
   under 0.6 deg in the 4 scored cycles - and 3 to 33 deg over 20. The heading per cycle showed
   why: e.g. straight075 -0.2, -1.2, -0.3, +0.1 (cycle 4), then +0.7, +2.2, +4.2 ... +33.3. The
   start of a walk is a transient that wobbles, and the GA found gaits whose wobble happens to end
   facing straight ahead at cycle 4; the steady turn that follows was never scored. Another loophole
   of the kind in section 3: what is not measured gets exploited.
3. Tune the steer gene alone (trainer option tune=<run>, outputs/straight0xx_tuned): walk the gait
   12 cycles with steer -0.3 ... 0.3 (7 values), score the STEADY turn rate (slope of the heading
   over cycles 4-12, the transient left out), then bracket the zero crossing (interpolation and
   bisection, the response is not linear: straight075 +1.9 deg/cycle at steer 0, -1.0 at 0.1,
   -1.8 at 0.2). Steer settled between +0.007 and +0.198, residual turn -0.64 to +0.35 deg/cycle.
   Over 20 cycles the tuned family turns -7.5 to +2.9 deg (radius 66 m to 6 km), against +1.6 to
   +33 deg (17-180 m) before. These are now the network's training data.
A longer GA evaluation (e.g. 10 cycles) would make step 3 part of the GA, but would cost 2.5x the
run time (the 4-cycle family already took ~3 hours with 9 runs in parallel).

Heading feedback when playing a gait (gait_player, controllerArgs [<run>, <period>, "straight"]).
The network's gaits still curved after retraining (up to 18 deg in 40 s): their joint-angle error
(~1.9 deg) is as large as the steer correction itself (~1-2 deg of hip swing), so open-loop
prediction cannot get the steer right. Real walking robots close the loop instead. The player
does the same with the steer mechanism: the first 2 cycles set the line, then once per cycle
steer = 1.0 x error + an integral term (0.5 x error per cycle, which learns each gait's steady
curve), where error = travel direction over the last cycle minus the direction towards the line
3 m ahead. The robot only has a GPS (an InertialUnit added to the proto crashed Webots on load),
so the heading is the direction of travel. 20 cycles, sideways offset at the end:
    gait                open loop   with feedback   forward (open -> feedback)
    nn_speed_0.117      -0.21 m     +0.05 m          2.82 -> 2.87 m
    nn_speed_0.240      -0.09       -0.03            5.13 -> 4.99
    nn_speed_0.300      +1.05       +0.03            6.27 -> 6.32
    nn_speed_0.357      -0.08       -0.01            7.82 -> 7.69
    nn_speed_0.400      -0.69       -0.02            8.71 -> 8.73
    nn_speed_0.451      -0.31       -0.01            9.73 -> 9.80
    ref10_2             +3.02       -0.00           10.0  -> 10.5
    pop80_s2           -12.5        -0.92 (oscillating, steer at its limit)   16.0 -> 10.6
Every gait but pop80_s2 holds its line within 5 cm without losing speed. pop80_s2 curves more
than the steer range (+-0.3) can cancel.

8. Foot-path encoding: the GA drives the IK network (encoding=foot, tilt=)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------
Every encoding above moves each joint along a sine, so the feet go wherever the joints take them,
and they slide (limitation below). Part 2's per-leg inverse-kinematics network (option E,
docs/ik_comparison.md) turns a foot position into its leg's 3 angles, so a gait can instead be
written the way a spider walks: each foot planted and still on the ground while the body passes
over it, then lifted and swung forward (part2_nn/ik/foot_gait.py). encoding=foot searches the 7
settings of those foot paths instead of joint sines:
    stride   0.10-0.60 m   how far a foot moves back during stance
    lift     0.04-0.30 m   swing height
    duty     0.45-0.80     share of the cycle a foot is on the ground
    height  -0.68--0.50 m  ground level below the body centre (lower is out of reach)
    reach    0.85-1.10     lateral foot distance, x pi0's average
    spread   0.85-1.15     front/hind foot distance along the body, x pi0's average
    wave     0.00-0.15     back-to-front ripple: each pair steps this share of a cycle after the
                           pair behind it (0 = two rigid tetrapods)
gait.decode sends a 7-gene chromosome to the network, so the trainer, plots, tune and replay work
unchanged. The swing leaves and lands at the stance's backward speed, so the foot is still over
the ground at lift-off and touch-down.

Tilt (option tilt=, rad): the tilt rule (section 3) penalises mean body pitch beyond `tilt`.
The default 0.07 (4 deg) keeps the old runs reproducible; these runs use 0.14 (8 deg), a slight
lean allowed.

Runs foot_t14_s0..2: pop 40, 50 generations, 4 cycles per evaluation (start transients matter
less than over 2), mutation rate 0.2 (section 4: best for small encodings), tilt 0.14.
    run           forward (4 cycles)  slide   pitch   20 cycles: forward, heading, sideways
    foot_t14_s1   4.10 m              3.30 m  0.015   20.30 m, +2.0 deg, +0.78 m
    foot_t14_s0   4.04 m              3.12 m  0.014   20.01 m, +3.0 deg, +1.01 m
    foot_t14_s2   3.80 m              4.30 m  0.020
    pi0           2.05 m              7.75 m  0.059
    best hand-set foot path (0.40 / 0.24 / 0.50)  2.53 m  5.91 m  0.022   14.61 m, -0.5 deg
* Twice pi0's distance with less than half its sliding, level (pitch 0.015, under even the old
  4 deg rule, so the relaxed tilt was not needed), no leg collisions, no fall. Over 20 cycles it
  beats the fastest joint-sine gait (pop80_s2, 16.0 m but curving 64 deg) and holds its heading
  within 2-3 deg.
* Seeds 0 and 1 found nearly the same genes independently (stride 0.60, lift 0.29-0.30, duty
  0.58, height -0.51/-0.52, reach 0.87, spread 1.13/1.15, wave 0.13): not a lucky seed.
* The GA chose a ripple (wave 0.13): the gait diagram (outputs/foot_t14_s1/foot_heights.png)
  shows each leg stepping just after the one behind it, as real spiders do, not two rigid groups.
* stride, lift, height and spread ended at or near their upper bounds, reach near its lower one:
  the best gait probably lies beyond these bounds; a wider run is the obvious next step.
* The hind feet dip ~2.5 cm below ground level at the end of stance (foot_heights.png): targets
  far back at that height are near the edge of reach.

9. Limitations
--------------
* Results vary a lot by seed: every configuration has a seed that only steps in place
  (0.0-0.3 m). 3 seeds per configuration is a small sample; the means above are rough.
* pi0 slides a lot (7.7 m for 2.05 m forward; refined ref10_2: 4.9 m for 1.94 m); shared1
  leans forward 12 deg.
* Feet slide in every joint-sine gait: one sine per joint cannot draw a flat stance stroke
  (the foot-path encoding, section 8, roughly halves it, but planted feet still slip under load).
* Each gait is scored over 2 cycles from a standing start; some looked fine over 2 cycles and
  sank or slowed over 4 (shared2), and most curve over 20 (section 7). Longer evaluations would
  catch that, at extra cost. Straight walking now comes from a separate steer tuning step and
  from feedback at playback, not from the GA alone.
* The footfall diagram in plots.py uses commanded angles in the body frame; measure= gives
  the real contacts.
